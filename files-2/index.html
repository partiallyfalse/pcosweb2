What is PCOS?

PCOS stands for Polycystic Ovary Syndrome. It's one of the most common hormonal conditions in women, affecting roughly 1 in 10 people. It's not rare at all bunny, it's not your fault at all, and it's super duper manageable once you understand what's going on, AANNNNDDD ALSOSOSOSOSOSO WHEN YOU HAVE MEEEEEE.

Bassicly, PCOS is a condition where hormones that normally work together in a specific way — estrogen, progesterone, and androgens — get a divorce and start drama. Most people with PCOS produce slightly higher androgen levels than typical, just like you little bubu, and many also have some degree of insulin resistance, meaning their body needs a little more insulin than usual to keep blood sugar steady.

The name lies to you though my bubu, however, I never do. (HEHHEHEHEEH) PCOS doesn't actually require cysts on the ovaries. The name comes from how ovaries can look on an ultrasound, sometimes they show many small fluid-filled follicles, but plenty of people with PCOS never even have this, and some people with those follicles don't have PCOS at all. So, just more shitty naming from everybody, but I think Mariam is a very super beautiful name.

How my little bunny probably was diagnosed

Doctors typically diagnose PCOS when at least two of these three show up to court:

  • Irregular or absent ovulation (which shows up as irregular periods)
  • Signs of higher androgen levels (in bloodwork or physical symptoms)
  • Polycystic-appearing ovaries on ultrasound

No one actually knows the exact single cause, go figure. It tends to run in families and seems to come from a mix of genetics, insulin response, and hormone regulation, so not from anything anyone did or didn't do.
